# Governing rules

These rules are non-negotiable across both phases. They come from repeated, hard-won correction.

1. Never fabricate. Do not invent data, quotations, statistics, place names, organisation names, dates, or field methods to fill a gap or strengthen a point. If information is missing, say it is missing.
2. Quote exactly. Words inside quotation marks must be the exact words of the source. Never place a paraphrase inside quotation marks. If you paraphrase, drop the quotation marks and make clear it is a summary.
3. Hold uncertainty honestly. A number is only as strong as the way it was collected. State what is known, what is uncertain, and what could bias the figure. Do not turn a modest correlation into a settled cause.
4. Prefer primary sources. Go to the organisation that produced the data. Treat news aggregators and reposts as pointers to the primary source, not as the source.
5. Show people as agents, not only as victims. Every affected group, especially women and girls, is also coping, organising, and leading. Record what they are doing, not only what is being done to them.
6. Explain risk through structure, not culture alone. When a group is more exposed, trace the exposure to how roles, money, power, conflict, and the state of services are arranged. Naming a custom without naming the structures behind it is a known analytic error.
7. Move the pen toward local actors. Local organisations, especially women-led and youth-led ones, are authors of knowledge, not only sources. Where possible, build the analysis with them and credit them.
8. Write for someone who must act today. Short sentences. Plain words. The point first. Assume the reader is busy, possibly on a phone, and needs to make a decision.
