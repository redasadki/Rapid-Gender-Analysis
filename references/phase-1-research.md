# Phase 1: research the emergency

Inputs: background documents, sitreps, and any other reference documents.

Produce a verified evidence base. Save it as a research file with full citations before moving on. For every claim, record the primary source and its date. Flag anything you could not verify.

## 1.1 Situation and scale

- What the emergency is, where, and since when.
- Current confirmed scale (cases, deaths, people affected, displacement) with the date of each figure.
- Trajectory: growing, stable, or declining. Geographic spread.
- Declaration status (for example a public health emergency of international concern, or a system-wide scale-up activation).

## 1.2 Disaggregated demographics

- Break the affected population down by sex and by age wherever the data allows. State exact figures and their date.
- Note where sex-disaggregated or age-disaggregated data is missing. That absence is itself a finding.
- Where two sources disagree on the split, report both. Do not resolve the disagreement by picking one.

## 1.3 Differential impact by gender and other axes

- How the emergency affects women, men, girls, and boys differently, and how gender intersects with disability, displacement status, age, ethnicity, occupation, and location.
- Specific exposure pathways for each group (for example caregiving, frontline health work, burial, water collection, transactional dependence).
- Effects on maternal and reproductive health, on gender-based violence (GBV), and on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) by responders.

## 1.4 Structural drivers and the pre-crisis baseline

- Conflict, economic, political, and health-system conditions that shape the emergency.
- State of services before the emergency: was maternal care, GBV response, or the health system already weak or under attack?
- Change over time: the situation of affected groups before the crisis, so the analysis shows what the crisis added.

## 1.5 Capacities, coping, and local leadership

- What affected communities, and especially women and local organisations, are already doing.
- Which local and women-led organisations are active, what they do, and how they are funded.
- Trusted information channels and community structures.

## 1.6 Evidence from comparable past emergencies

- Where current local data is thin, gather evidence from comparable past emergencies in the same region or of the same type.
- Label it clearly as historical or comparative evidence, not current local data.

## 1.7 What the humanitarian system is already saying

- Existing gender analyses, situation reports, cluster guidance, and statements from UN agencies and NGOs.
- If an existing RGA or gender brief exists, read the full document, not the summary. Record what it recommends and whom it names to act.

## Verification gate

Complete all four items before Phase 2.

1. List every source with its URL and date.
2. Mark each key claim as verified (two or more independent sources, or one primary source), single-source, or unverified.
3. State the three biggest gaps in the evidence.
4. Hand the file to the operator for correction.

## Evidence file layout

```
# Evidence base: [emergency], [date]
## Scope and search log (queries, dates searched, languages)
## 1.1 to 1.7 findings (bullets, each with source number and date)
## Claim verification table (claim / status / sources)
## Three biggest gaps
## Sources (number, organisation, title, date, URL)
```
