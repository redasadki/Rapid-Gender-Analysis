# Phase 2: write the Rapid Gender Analysis

Inputs: the Phase 1 evidence file, and any operator corrections or instructions.

Turn the verified evidence into a complete RGA. Meet every structural element for which you have evidence. Where evidence is missing, state the gap plainly.

## Document opening

Before the executive summary, write one short institutional statement naming the producing organisation, the date of production, and the method basis (for example: secondary data review of primary sources published between [date] and [date]). State the citation format in use. No YAML front matter, metadata block, or front matter of any kind.

## Formatting rules

- Headings: short sentences that carry the argument. Write "Maternal health was in crisis before the emergency and the loss of staff leaves pregnant women without care", not "Maternal health". Implications go in the first body sentence.
- Citations for web publication: inline hyperlinks on the claim phrase itself, for example the figure "837 confirmed cases and 196 deaths" is the link.
- Citations for PDF, print, or messaging: a numbered references section at the end, with self-contained body text and no inline markers.
- Never mix the two citation formats.
- Body: one to two sentence prose paragraphs for interpretation. Bullets for parallel facts. Conclusion first, evidence after.
- For each group or theme: impact, exposure pathway, structural driver in the same sentence as any custom or role, change against the pre-crisis baseline, and the group as agents.

## Recommendation format

Each recommendation uses four labelled sub-items. Never embed them in prose.

```
- Actor: [who carries it out]
- Action: [the concrete step]
- Link to finding: [the evidence this answers]
- Budget line or decision seat: [how it is funded or where it sits]
```

## Required structure

### A. Executive summary

- First sentence: the single most important finding, the one that should change a decision.
- Headline sex-disaggregated figures with their date, or a plain statement that they do not exist.
- Sub-headers when covering more than two types of content, for example "What is confirmed", "What field reports already show", "Recommendations based on these findings".
- Recommendations that remain actionable if read out of context.
- Close with the limits of the analysis and when it must be updated.

### B. Situation and gendered impact

Thematic sections with argument-carrying headings. Cover as distinct threads:

- maternal and reproductive health
- gender-based violence
- protection from sexual exploitation and abuse by responders
- children and family separation
- the government or authority as partner and potential obstacle
- a section whose heading signals whose knowledge produced the analysis, for example "The analysis was built from secondary data, not with affected communities, which limits its validity"

### C. Recommendations

Keep the list short and ordered by urgency, not sector. The set must include:

1. Unrestricted funding directly to named local women-led organisations without a competitive proposal process.
2. A formal decision-making seat (not observer status) for local women-led organisations in coordination structures.
3. Sex and age disaggregation in data collection and reporting.
4. A follow-up RGA co-authored with local women-led organisations.

### D. Dissemination

A table with four columns: Audience, Format, Channel, Responsibility. Explain how the analysis reaches local partners and affected communities. The community version must be produced by local organisations in the local language, not miniaturised from the international document. Name channels actually in use (for example WhatsApp, community radio, church networks, displacement tracking sites).

### E. Limitations

After the recommendations, before the references. Name:

- the data basis
- who was not consulted
- whose knowledge is credited and how
- the risk that visible organisations do not represent the full local response
- whether the framework (IASC clusters, standard RGA tools) matches local priorities
- whether any resources, decision authority, or credit moved to local actors, and if not, why not

Add a short "Learn more:" pointer with a link where a methodological resource helps.

### F. References

Numbered. Every cited source, then a sub-header for background documents consulted but not cited. Full entries: author or organisation, title, publication or platform, date, visible URL.
